Data-Efficient Humanoid Loco-Manipulation Learning via Heterogeneous Human Data Pretraining and Iterative Offline RL

Anonymous Authors · ICRA 2027 submission

Abstract

Learning whole-body loco-manipulation from teleoperated robot data is costly and hard to scale. Human data offers a far cheaper and more diverse option for robot skill learning: it is already available at scale and used successfully for table-top bimanual robot arms. Transferring this success to humanoids is harder: the visual and kinematic gap is wider than for table-top bimanual arms. Most human data captures the head and both wrists' trajectories but not the whole-body motion that the low-level tracker for humanoids takes as input. This data is also stored in different formats, and different datasets have very different observation spaces and action spaces. In this work, we propose a new policy training recipe for learning humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation skills, in which we pretrain our policy by co-training on a large amount of human data that is canonicalized into the same action space, and post-train the policy with human-in-the-loop iterative offline RL. Our key insight is that a large amount of in-the-wild human data provides distributional coverage for the policy to learn how to accomplish the whole-body manipulation task in different scenarios, and a small amount of on-robot human-in-the-loop post-training shapes and sharpens that distribution to achieve a higher success rate in accomplishing hard loco-manipulation tasks in the wild. We demonstrate how our recipe performs in selected whole-body manipulation tasks that span the demands of whole-body loco-manipulation: our trained policy reaches a 20/20 success rate on the any-object pickup task with language conditioning and whole-body posture coordination, measured in unseen environments throughout. Our policy also achieves 17/20 and 16/20 success rates on pushing and pulling doors with heavy door knobs. The recipe works effectively in tasks involving whole-body contact like torso-assisted whole-body box carrying, and achieves a 16/20 success rate in unseen environments.

    1 · Data preparation

    Canonicalize human data in different formats into the same action space

    Human data only records the head and both wrists' trajectories. A motion completion model first completes the full-body motion for the high-level policy to learn the behavior. VLM agents are involved in the loop to make final fine-grained tweaks to individual motions, so they look natural, smooth and easier for the low-level whole-body tracker to track. The humanoid low-level controller encodes the whole-body motion into FSQ tokens.

    • Motion completion (Kimodo): 3 tracked points (head + two grippers) in, 29-DoF whole-body G1 motion out, 30 → 50 Hz
    • One action space: SONIC's encoder maps the motion to a 64-D FSQ code: every dimension is a scalar snapped to a 1/16 lattice, so each traces a staircase over time
    • Scale: 10 h of human data per door or box task; 1,500 h for pickup
    2 · Pretraining

    Heterogeneous pretraining on human data in different formats

    An ACT policy is co-trained on all human data to predict SONIC's FSQ coordinates; the frozen SONIC decoder turns the predicted FSQ coordinates into whole-body joint PD targets. No robot data is used throughout the pretraining stage.

    • Inputs: head and wrist cameras, wrist orientation and velocity, distance between wrists
    • Head views are heavily randomized and masked due to the human–robot visual gap: the human head view is masked and noised, so the policy learns from the wrist camera views whenever possible
    • Rates: the ACT policy predicts 2 s action chunks at 1 Hz; the SONIC whole-body tracker executes 1 s of each predicted chunk at 50 Hz
    3 · Post-training

    Twenty minutes of human-in-the-loop offline RL

    In each round, the current policy runs on the robot while an operator watches and takes over only when it strays: in VR, or by guiding the grippers by hand from behind the robot, keeping their hands out of the wrist cameras' view. That round's rollouts and corrections are added to the robot data collected so far, combined with the pretraining human data, and used to post-train the previous round's policy into the next one. Takeovers shrink round by round.

    • Critic: IQL scores every action chunk; AWR imitates corrections more and chunks that led to a takeover less
    • Co-training: every update mixes all robot data so far with the pretraining human data (kept at weight one), keeping the coverage direct finetuning forgets
    • Budget: 4 rounds × 5 min = 20 min of robot data per task; takeovers fall from ~10% to under 2%
    • Result: all six task variants reach 80–100% in unseen environments (see Results)

    Results

    Every training recipe below gets the same twenty minutes of robot time and is tested in four environments the robot has never seen, five trials each. Pretraining on diverse human data plus a short round of on-robot correction beats every alternative: learning from robot demonstrations alone, pretraining on narrower human data, and post-training without a learned critic.

    Successful trials out of 20 per task in the four unseen environments; any-object pickup pools three objects, so it is out of 60. "Learned critic" means the post-training weighs each logged robot action by how useful the critic judges it to be.

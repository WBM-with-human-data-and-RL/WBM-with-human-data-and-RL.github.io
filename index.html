Data-Efficient Humanoid Loco-Manipulation Learning via Heterogeneous Human Data Pretraining and Iterative Offline RL

Abstract

Humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation is usually learned from teleoperated robot data, which is commonly known to be costly to collect and hard to scale. Human data offers a far cheaper and more diverse option for robot skill learning. But due to the strong visual and kinematic gap between humans and humanoids, researchers have long struggled to make use of those scaled data already collected in bulk that works for table-top bimanual arms. Those data are also saved in different formats, and different dataset have very different observation spaces and action spaces. In this work, we propose a new policy training recipe for humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation, which we pretrain our policy by co-training large amount of human data that are formatted into the same action space, and post-train the policy with human-in-the-loop iterative offline RL. Our key insight is that a large amount of human data in the wild provides distributional coverage for the policy to learn how to accomplish the whole-body manipulation task in different scenarios, and a small amount of on-robot human-in-the-loop post-training shapes and sharpens that distribution to achieve a higher success rate in accomplishing hard loco-manipulation tasks in the wild. We demonstrate how our recipe performs in selected whole-body manipulation tasks that span the demands of whole body loco-manipulation: our trained policy reaches a 20/20 success rate in the any-object pickup task with language conditioning and whole body posture coordination, measured in unseen environments throughout. Our policy also achieves 16/20 and 17/20 success rates pushing and pulling door opening tasks with doors with door handle weights. The recipe works effectively well in tasks involving whole-body contact like torso-assisted whole-body box carrying, and achieves a 16/20 success rate in held-out environments. Additional training recipe's introduction and experimental results are available at https://wbm-with-human-data-and-rl.github.io/.
